## 🔴 CRITICAL FIXES

### 1. Synthetic-Only Evaluation (Biggest Weakness)
**Problem:**
All 1,200 evaluation sessions were synthetically generated from just 6 behavioral personas using structured templates. The paper itself admits:
> *"the High Precision@5 (0.76) and NDCG@5 (0.77) should therefore be treated as upper-bound estimates"*

Every serious reviewer will flag this. Synthetic data generated by the same team that built the system is easily inflated.

**What to fix:**
- Collect at least **50–100 real career counseling conversations** from actual students
- Re-run Precision@5, NDCG@5, and MRR on the real data
- Report both synthetic and real-world results side by side
- Even if real-world numbers are lower (e.g., 0.65 vs 0.76), showing it still beats baselines on real data is far more convincing

**Where in the paper:**
- Section V-G (Synthetic Conversational Dataset)
- Section VI (Experiments) — add Experiment 9: Real-World Pilot
- Section VII-J (Limitations) — currently acknowledged, needs to be resolved

---

### 2. Only 30 Careers in Hand-Annotated Set
**Problem:**
The paper ranks 30 careers. Precision@5 over 30 items is a much easier task than real production systems with 300+ careers. The paper acknowledges:
> *"with only 30 hand-annotated occupational profiles, the ranking problem is easier than in production systems"*

**What to fix:**
- Expand from 30 to at least **60–80 hand-annotated careers**
- Full annotation of the existing 74 Kaggle-derived careers (add Ikigai mappings + market data to them — they currently only have OCEAN profiles)
- Re-run all ranking metrics on the larger set
- The 104-career secondary layer exists but is incomplete — finish it

**Where in the paper:**
- Section V-A (Career Database)
- Table VI (Career Dataset Statistics)
- Table XIX (Full 30-Career Database) — needs to become Table XIX (Full 60+ Career Database)

---

### 3. Career Prediction Dataset — Single Sample Per Career
**Problem:**
The Kaggle Career Prediction dataset has only **1 row per career** (105 rows, 104 careers). This means OCEAN profiles are point estimates with zero standard deviation. Blending these 50/50 with hand-coded profiles introduces noise, not signal.

The paper admits this:
> *"the dataset provides only one sample per career label for most occupations, yielding effectively zero standard deviation for most OCEAN profiles"*

**What to fix:**
- Find a better OCEAN-career mapping dataset with multiple samples per career
- Or: collect additional samples via the 45-participant user study participants (after enough sessions, their converged profiles + stated career goals give you real OCEAN-career pairs)
- At minimum, add a sensitivity analysis: show results with and without the 50/50 blend to prove the blend actually helps

**Where in the paper:**
- Section V-C (Career Prediction Dataset)
- Section III-F-2 (Psychometric Match equation 9)

## 🟡 IMPORTANT FIXES

### 4. User Study is Too Small and Too Homogeneous
**Problem:**
- Only **45 participants**, age 18–28, India only, undergraduate/postgraduate
- No control group (no baseline group using a traditional career tool like RIASEC for comparison)
- 2-week window is short for "longitudinal" claims
- No follow-up: did participants actually pursue recommended careers?

**What to fix:**
- Increase to at least **80–100 participants** if possible
- Add a **control group** — give some participants a standard RIASEC questionnaire and compare satisfaction
- Extend observation to **4–6 weeks** to actually validate the longitudinal claim
- Add one follow-up question: "Did this recommendation change your career thinking?" (even 1 month later)
- Report demographic breakdown in a table

**Where in the paper:**
- Section VI (Experiment 8: User Satisfaction)
- Table XVIII (User Satisfaction Scores)

---

### 5. Missing Ablation on the 50/50 Blend Weight
**Problem:**
The psychometric match uses a fixed 50/50 blend of hand-coded and data-driven OCEAN profiles. There is no experiment showing why 50/50 is optimal vs 30/70 or 70/30.

The composite score weights (w1=0.40, w2=0.35, w3=0.25) are grid-searched (Table X), but the 50/50 blend inside Psych(c) is never ablated.

**What to fix:**
- Add a grid search over blend ratios: 100/0, 70/30, 50/50, 30/70, 0/100
- Report NDCG@5 for each — prove 50/50 is actually optimal
- Add this as Experiment 9 or a subsection of the ablation

**Where in the paper:**
- Section VI (Experiments) — new sub-experiment
- Section III-F-2 (Psychometric Match)

---

### 6. No Baseline Comparison with a Conversational AI System
**Problem:**
The baselines are:
1. GPT-4 zero-shot (no conversation history, no profiling)
2. Holland RIASEC (questionnaire-based)
3. Random baseline

There is no comparison with another **conversational career guidance system** or even a simpler chatbot baseline (e.g., GPT-4 with conversation history but no EWMA/Ikigai).

**What to fix:**
- Add baseline: **GPT-4 with conversation history** (no EWMA, no Ikigai, no market data)
- This directly proves each component of ELEVARE adds value beyond just "having a conversation"
- This is already partially covered by the ablation but should be a named baseline

**Where in the paper:**
- Section VI (Experiment 1: Performance Comparison)
- Table XI (Performance comparison table)

### 7. Latency Numbers Missing from Abstract and Results Summary
**Problem:**
The paper mentions a latency experiment (Experiment 6, Table XVI) but the actual latency numbers are not visible in the key results sections. For a production system, reviewers at systems-focused venues care about this.

**What to fix:**
- Add mean end-to-end latency (ms) to the abstract or introduction
- Include p99 latency, not just mean
- Report latency breakdown: NLP pipeline vs EWMA update vs LLM call vs recommendation scoring

**Where in the paper:**
- Abstract (add 1 sentence: "End-to-end latency of X ms supports real-time deployment")
- Section VI (Experiment 6)

### 10. Cross-Cultural Validation of Ikigai
**Problem:**
Ikigai is a Japanese philosophical concept. The paper is built by Indian researchers, evaluated on Indian students, with Indian salary data (INR). There is zero validation that the Ikigai framework maps correctly to non-Japanese career values.

The paper acknowledges: *"cross-cultural validation of the Ikigai alignment module is needed before non-Japanese-context deployment"*

**What to fix:**
- Either: explicitly scope the paper to India/South Asia context in the abstract and title
- Or: add a paragraph in Section VII discussing whether the 4 Ikigai dimensions (Love, Good At, World Needs, Paid For) are culturally universal
- Cite cross-cultural career satisfaction research to support or challenge this

**Where in the paper:**
- Abstract (add geographic scope)
- Section VII (Limitations — currently mentioned briefly, needs more depth)

### 14. Equation Numbering — Eq. (8) is the Composite Score, Not Eq. (9)
**Problem:**
In Section III-F, the equations are:
- Eq. (7) = Market Score M(c)
- Eq. (8) = Composite Score (listed as the main formula)
- Eq. (9) = Psychometric Match Psych(c)

But Algorithm 1 (line 13) references `Score(c) = 0.40 Psych(c) + 0.35 A(c) + 0.25 M(c)` without an equation number, while the abstract and introduction reference "Eq. (8)" for the composite score.

**Fix:** Verify equation numbering is consistent throughout — especially the key formula referenced in the abstract.
